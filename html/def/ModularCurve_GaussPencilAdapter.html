Gauss subring, orders and residue algebras for prolongation pairs

Throughout, q is a prime, A a valuation subring of \overline{\mathbb Q}, k a field of characteristic q with a ring map red : A \to k, and P a PlaceSpecialization attached to modular polynomial data data satisfying a Kronecker congruence together with integrality hypotheses h\alpha,h\beta for the two Hecke branches; R is a LevelOneProlongationPair P, so R carries two regular prolongations R_1,R_2 of A to the geometric level-q function field F= modularFunctionFieldBar (1 * q) with residue field modularFunctionFieldFullC (ResidueField A) 1, together with a map \iota of that residue field into modularFunctionFieldC k 1. Finally K_1 is a field sitting in a tower \overline{\mathbb Q} \subseteq K_1 \subseteq F (given by algebra structures and a scalar tower).

The basic object is gaussBase: the valuation subring V = R_1 \cap K_1 of K_1, obtained by pulling back R_1.integers along K_1 \to F. Its membership criterion is recorded, as is the fact that a constant c \in \overline{\mathbb Q} lies in V exactly when c \in A. The Prop-valued class HasGaussTransport asserts that R_1 and R_2 cut out the same subring of K_1, in the form of the two implications for images of elements of K_1. gaussOrder y f is the V-subalgebra V[y,f] of F generated by two elements; it is shown to lie in R_1.integers (and, under HasGaussTransport, in R_2.integers) whenever y,f do, via a general statement that a V-adjunction lands in a valuation subring containing V and the generators.

ResOne and ResTwo are two copies of the level-one field modularFunctionFieldFullC (ResidueField A) 1, with identity-valued coercions and ring equivalences to it; they are made V-algebras by resBase₁ = R₁.residue ∘ toIntegers₁ and resBase₂ respectively, where toIntegers₁, toIntegers₂ are the restrictions of K_1 \to F to V. Both resBase₁ and resBase₂ are local homomorphisms (units are reflected, proved from a general reflection lemma for maps V \to O compatible with the inclusion of K_1), hence induce algebra structures of \mathrm{ResidueField}(V) on ResOne, ResTwo, with the corresponding scalar towers; compatibility with constants from A is recorded. Further maps: the inclusions gaussOrderToIntegers₁/₂ of V[y,f] into the two valuation rings, the V-algebra maps \rho_1,\rho_2 : V[y,f] \to ResOne, ResTwo given by the two residue maps, the local homomorphism constToGaussBase : A \to V with the induced map \mathrm{ResidueField}(A) \to \mathrm{ResidueField}(V), and \Psi_1,\Psi_2, the maps from ResOne, ResTwo to modularFunctionFieldC k 1 given by \iota. A preliminary section re-declares, at high priority, the canonical Field, Algebra and Module instances on Laurent series over \overline{\mathbb Q} and on the modular function fields.

Relation to Mathlib

Built on Mathlib's ValuationSubring.comap, Algebra.adjoin, and IsLocalRing.ResidueField.lift/.map; the prolongation pairs and modular function field towers are the project's own notions, and ResOne/ResTwo are type copies of one of them rather than new mathematical objects.

Where it is used

These definitions supply the vocabulary in which the reduction of functions on the geometric level-q modular curve is compared, along a pencil of functions defined over an intermediate field K_1, with the two branches R_1,R_2 of the prolongation pair; statements elsewhere in the development about norms from F to K_1 and about summed pencil laws are phrased with V=R_1\cap K_1, the orders V[y,f] and the maps \rho_1,\rho_2. They belong to the analysis of the two-component special fibre at q used in the study of the specialisation of J_0(q) entering level lowering.

    (hV : ∀ r : R.gaussBase K₁, algebraMap (R.gaussBase K₁) (modularFunctionFieldBar (1 * q)) r ∈ O)
    {s : Set (modularFunctionFieldBar (1 * q))} (hs : ∀ y ∈ s, y ∈ O) :
    ∀ x ∈ Algebra.adjoin (R.gaussBase K₁) s, x ∈ O := by
  intro x hx
  induction hx using Algebra.adjoin_induction with
  | mem y hy => exact hs y hy
  | algebraMap r => exact hV r
  | add y z _ _ hy hz => exact add_mem hy hz
  | mul y z _ _ hy hz => exact mul_mem hy hz

theorem isUnit_of_isUnit_toIntegers {O : ValuationSubring (modularFunctionFieldBar (1 * q))}
    (g : R.gaussBase K₁ →+* O)
    (hg : ∀ x : R.gaussBase K₁, (g x : modularFunctionFieldBar (1 * q)) = algebraMap K₁ _ (x : K₁))
    (hO : ∀ x : K₁, algebraMap K₁ (modularFunctionFieldBar (1 * q)) x ∈ O → x ∈ R.gaussBase K₁)
    (x : R.gaussBase K₁) (hu : IsUnit (g x)) : IsUnit x := by
  obtain ⟨u, hu⟩ := hu
  have hinv : ((u⁻¹ : Oˣ) : O) * g x = 1 := by rw [← hu, Units.inv_mul]
  have hinvF : (((u⁻¹ : Oˣ) : O) : modularFunctionFieldBar (1 * q)) * algebraMap K₁ _ (x : K₁) = 1 := by
    have := congrArg (fun z : O => (z : modularFunctionFieldBar (1 * q))) hinv
    simpa [hg] using this
  have hx0 : (x : K₁) ≠ 0 := by
    rintro h
    rw [h, map_zero, mul_zero] at hinvF
    exact zero_ne_one hinvF
  have hxinv : (x : K₁)⁻¹ ∈ R.gaussBase K₁ := by
    apply hO
    rw [map_inv₀, ← eq_inv_of_mul_eq_one_left hinvF]
    exact ((u⁻¹ : Oˣ) : O).2
  refine isUnit_iff_exists_inv.mpr ⟨⟨_, hxinv⟩, Subtype.ext ?_⟩
  show (x : K₁) * (x : K₁)⁻¹ = 1
  exact mul_inv_cancel₀ hx0
